# Naming the desktop product

Checked 2026-08-23. **Every name below was checked against the live registry,
not guessed.** Prior naming work exists at `~/Documents/Juno/one-word-names-2026-08-19.md`
and is NOT the same brief — that one sells confidentiality to licensed
professionals. This is a desktop agentic assistant for an owner paying roughly
$90–170 a month. Different buyer, different name. Do not merge them.

## The recommendation: Remembrancer

`remembrancer.ai` — **FREE**, confirmed by whois.

**Why it is the word.** The King's Remembrancer is a real office of the English
courts, and the oldest judicial position in England still in existence. The job,
literally, is to remember what is owed to the Crown so the Crown does not have
to.

That is not a decorative etymology. It is the product's own closing line, which
has been in `CLAUDE.md` since 2026-08-09 and was written before anyone went
looking for a name:

> You'd stop being the person who has to remember.

It also passes the structural test the earlier naming work established: a
metaphor at one remove, borrowed from another domain, describing a
**disposition** rather than the product. Nobody can guess "desktop app that
drives an agent" from it — same mechanism as Anthropic, Palantir, Stripe.

**Its two honest problems.** Five syllables, which is long for the family it is
imitating. And it is not a word most Americans know, so it has to teach itself —
which the founding story does in one sentence, but only if someone tells it.

## The runner-up: Lodestral

`lodestral.com` AND `lodestral.ai` — **both FREE.** The only name in the whole
sweep where both are open.

A coinage off *lodestar* — the star you steer by. Three syllables, soft ending,
half-recognisable root, which is the Vercel pattern done properly rather than a
real word with a letter swapped. Weaker meaning than Remembrancer and stronger
availability. **If owning both TLDs matters more than the story, this is the
pick.**

## What the sweep actually proved, so nobody re-runs it

**Sixty-plus candidates checked. Every single dictionary word is gone in .com** —
cairn, orrery, tessera, continuo, auspex, vellum, kestrel, halcyon, meridian,
praxis, factotum, quorum, tacit, verity, all of them. So are the household-office
metaphors: seneschal, majordomo, chatelaine, remembrancer, understudy, almoner,
ostler, stillroom, anteroom.

**And so are the short coinages.** Thirty-six invented three-syllable names —
amanu, tenari, custor, orrel, vigel, mnemar, auspera, tacita, solvara — and
exactly one was free. Squatters hold the short .com space completely. **Do not
spend another hour there; the answer will be the same.**

`.ai` is where the space actually is, and for this product it is arguably the
better TLD anyway — the competitor sits on a .com and it does them no favours.
